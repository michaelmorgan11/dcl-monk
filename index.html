01 · CASH APPLICATION

One seat, a whole day, every day

A bank file is pulled each morning, remittances are hunted in the shared inbox, and matches are made by hand against NetSuite. The work runs a day or two in arrears. NetSuite's own cash application module was tried inside the last twelve months and abandoned.

“It was a disaster. It was just guessing.”
02 · BILLING ACCURACY

Rates match at billing, then drift

Rates are verified against the customer TMS before the invoice goes out. A week or two later the DOE diesel average moves and the customer's rate changes underneath it. The discrepancy surfaces only at cash application, often past the 60 or 90 day correction window.

“Ninety percent of them are fuel related.”
03 · TEAM CAPACITY

The book grows faster than the team

Two collectors cover the entire book, touching each account every other week at best. One specialist applies all the cash. The business is hiring 50 or more people over 18 months, almost all of it front office.

“We’ve got another ten to fifteen thousand loads to go. We’re getting close.”
04 · VISIBILITY

The weekly meeting runs off Excel

Two collectors write notes into a shared spreadsheet that keeps growing. Missing remittances are tracked as “requested remit” in the same file. Aging buckets show the balance but not the reason behind it.

“Instead of us having to go manually find where the problems are.”

The discrepancy is found at cash application. It is created at billing.

Every day between the rate moving and your team seeing it is a day closer to a correction window closing. Monk re-checks the customer's TMS on a schedule after you bill, isolates the charge that moved, and routes it to rebill while there is still time to collect it.

The operating model

Three connections. NetSuite stays the system of record.

A two-way NetSuite sync across both subsidiaries, a read-only Bank of America feed through Plaid or Atler, and the remittance inbox. Monk writes the payment object and the transaction record, never the invoice record, so there is one source of truth and nothing to reconcile afterwards.

The remittances your team called headaches.

Grief and Kerastar, jumbled

Grief pays on its own reference numbers rather than DCL invoice numbers, and Grief and Kerastar loads are mixed together down the remittance with nothing in the number to tell them apart. Long remits take real time to dissect.

Monk learns the reference-number format and the parent and child split once, then applies it to every remittance after, tying each customer total out before anything posts.
Ryder Share, one line, two codes

Pabst pays through Ryder Share. Line haul and fuel sit on one customer code, accessorials on another with an A suffix. The remittance combines them on a single line, so the split has to be worked out from open AR before cash can be recorded.

Monk reads the code suffix, pulls the open AR for both, and proposes the split with the reason shown. It will not post a payment it cannot tie out.
Third-party payers, bundled

Payment vendors settle three or more customers in a single payment. Separating it, totalling each customer and applying them individually is the single biggest time sink in the day.

Monk splits the bundle by customer, matches each total to its own open invoices, and surfaces only the portion it cannot resolve.
No remittance at all

The same handful of customers never send one. Today that becomes a note on a shared spreadsheet and a wait that can run days.

Monk triggers a playbook to that customer's AP within hours of the unmatched payment landing, and the thread stays visible in Outlook.

Trajectory

Why the match rate compounds.

NetSuite's native matching plateaus because it only ever checks a name and an amount. Monk starts above that and keeps climbing, because every exception your team resolves becomes a rule that resolves it automatically the next time.

ERP native, 40 to 60% Monk, 65% rising past 90% Week one, go live
Layer one Your own patterns, set by your team: name variants, code splits, multi-invoice settlements. Built once from your real remittances, then applied forever. Monk suggests the rule from the match your team just made.
Layer two Deterministic checks on amount, customer name, memo and timing. This is the layer NetSuite stops at. Clears the easy majority with no model involved and no chance of a creative answer.
Layer three The investigative work a person does now: read the remit, read the email, scan open AR, decide. Proposes a match with a confidence score and the reason shown, so the thinking can be spot checked.
The guardrail Leslie's stated fear: that an automated system starts applying things wrong. Nothing auto-applies below 100% confidence. Everything else waits in a review queue. A wrong application costs more than a slow one.
