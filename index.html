01 · CASH APPLICATION

One seat, a whole day, every day

A bank file is pulled each morning, remittances are hunted in the shared inbox, and matches are made by hand against NetSuite. The work runs a day or two in arrears. NetSuite's own cash application module was tried inside the last twelve months and abandoned.

“It was a disaster. It was just guessing.”
02 · BILLING ACCURACY

Rates match at billing, then drift

Rates are verified against the customer TMS before the invoice goes out. A week or two later the DOE diesel average moves and the customer's rate changes underneath it. The discrepancy surfaces only at cash application, often past the 60 or 90 day correction window.

“Ninety percent of them are fuel related.”
03 · TEAM CAPACITY

The book grows faster than the team

Two collectors cover the entire book, touching each account every other week at best. One specialist applies all the cash. The business is hiring 50 or more people over 18 months, almost all of it front office.

“We’ve got another ten to fifteen thousand loads to go. We’re getting close.”
04 · VISIBILITY

The weekly meeting runs off Excel

Two collectors write notes into a shared spreadsheet that keeps growing. Missing remittances are tracked as “requested remit” in the same file. Aging buckets show the balance but not the reason behind it.

“Instead of us having to go manually find where the problems are.”

The discrepancy is found at cash application. It is created at billing.

Every day between the rate moving and your team seeing it is a day closer to a correction window closing. Monk re-checks the customer's TMS on a schedule after you bill, isolates the charge that moved, and routes it to rebill while there is still time to collect it.

The algorithm

Three layers, and two gates before a dollar moves.

Most cash application tools are one layer: reference plus amount, and everything else lands on a person. NetSuite's module is that layer, which is why the trial last year ended where it did. Monk runs three layers in strict priority order, and the cheap deterministic ones go first.

TIER 1 · YOUR RULES

The tribal knowledge, written down

What lives in one person's head today, executed automatically. A rule keys off whatever actually identifies the payment: the payer name, a reference on the remittance, the amount, or a pattern in your own numbering.

It can point a payment at one customer, split it across several, or take a transaction out of the queue entirely.
TIER 2 · DETERMINISTIC

Reference and amount, with the gates native matching lacks

The layer NetSuite stops at, done properly. Monk extracts order and BOL tokens from both the remittance and the bank memo, tests multiple number formats against open invoices, and scores the payer across the full parent and child hierarchy, legal names included.

No model is called. A clean payment closes without one.
TIER 3 · AI INFERENCE

Only what the first two cannot close

Reading an unstructured PDF remittance and pulling line-level detail. Splitting one third-party deposit across three or more customers. Recognising that a payment covers eleven of the fourteen invoices on a statement, and naming which three are missing.

Output is a proposal with a confidence score and written reasoning, never a silent posting.

What happens when it is not sure.

This is the part that separates it from what your team tried last year. Nothing auto-applies until a proposal clears two independent gates, and the second one does not trust the model at all.

Gate one · confidence

Every invoice in the match has to score at or above a threshold your team sets, not a threshold Monk sets. One weak invoice in an otherwise clean proposal stops the whole posting.

Your dial. Start it strict, which is what Leslie asked for, and loosen it only once the match rate has earned it.
Gate two · settlement

The stronger of the two, and it is pure arithmetic. Monk discards the model's math entirely and recomputes the allocation from its own ledger. Every invoice has to be covered exactly, either by cash or by an explicitly booked adjustment.

No unexplained gaps. A proposal that clears confidence but leaves a gap of any size is routed to review instead of posted.
